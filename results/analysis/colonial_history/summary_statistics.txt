Experiment 4a: Hong Kong vs Macao Comparison
================================================================================

Design: Natural experiment comparing colonial language effects
- Hong Kong (British colony 1842-1997): English advantage
- Macao (Portuguese colony 1557-1999): Portuguese advantage

Hypothesis: Hong Kong's English advantage > Macao's Portuguese advantage

Results:
--------
Hong Kong English advantage: 15.84%
  - English (en-native) distance: 1.5593
  - Chinese (zh-cn) distance: 1.8528
  - Number of models: 20

Macao Portuguese advantage: -15.67%
  - Portuguese (pt) distance: 1.5055
  - Chinese (zh-cn) distance: 1.3015
  - Number of models: 20

Statistical Test:
-----------------
Independent-samples t-test (Welch's correction)
  t(37.9) = 0.509
  p = 0.6138 (not significant)
  Cohen's d = 0.161 (negligible effect)
  Mean difference = 28.14%
  95% CI of difference: [-83.81, 140.09]

Interpretation:
---------------
The difference between Hong Kong's English advantage and Macao's Portuguese 
advantage is not statistically significant. High variance across models 
(Hong Kong SD=179.23%, Macao SD=170.37%) prevents us from concluding a 
significant difference.

Experiment 4b: Latin American Language Variants
================================================================================

Design: Compare English advantage across colonial language groups
- Spanish-speaking countries (n=11)
- Portuguese-speaking (Brazil, n=1)
- French-speaking (Haiti, n=1)

Hypothesis: English advantage varies by colonial language

Results:
--------
Spanish-speaking countries (n=11):
  Mean English advantage: 5.43%
  SD: 10.17%
  95% CI: [-0.58, 11.43]

Portuguese-speaking (Brazil, n=1):
  Mean English advantage: 3.58%

French-speaking (Haiti, n=1):
  Mean English advantage: 20.38%

Statistical Test:
-----------------
One-Way ANOVA
  F(2, 10) = 1.035
  p = 0.3905 (not significant)
  eta-squared = 0.171 (large effect size)

Interpretation:
---------------
No significant differences found across colonial language groups. Despite 
numerical differences and a large effect size, the small sample sizes 
(especially for Portuguese n=1 and French n=1) prevent us from concluding 
significant differences.

Experiment 4c: African Colonial History Effects
================================================================================

Design: Compare English advantage between African countries with and without
British colonial history

CRITICAL DATA LIMITATION DETECTED:
-----------------------------------
All British-colonized African countries in our dataset (Kenya, Nigeria, Ghana, 
South Africa, Zimbabwe, Zambia) only have English (en-native) language data. 
They lack native language data (Swahili, Yoruba, Akan, Zulu, Shona, Bemba, etc.).

Impact:
-------
The English advantage metric requires BOTH English and native language data:
    English Advantage (%) = 100 × (Distance_native - Distance_english) / Distance_native

Without native language data, we CANNOT calculate English advantage for 
British-colonized African countries, which means we CANNOT test the original 
hypothesis.

Recommendation:
---------------
This should be acknowledged as a methodological limitation in the paper. 
Future work should collect native language data for these countries to enable 
proper colonial history analysis in the African context.

Alternative Data:
-----------------
French-colonized African countries with native language data:
  Morocco (Arabic): English advantage = 18.01%
  Algeria (Arabic): English advantage = 10.83%

Note: These cannot be compared with British-colonized countries because 
British-colonized countries lack native language data.

Key Findings
================================================================================

1. Hong Kong shows positive English advantage (15.84%), but the comparison 
   with Macao is not statistically significant due to high variance.

2. Latin American colonial language groups show numerical differences in 
   English advantage, but these are not statistically significant due to 
   small sample sizes.

3. African colonial history analysis cannot be performed as designed due to 
   missing native language data for British-colonized countries.

4. High variance across models is a consistent challenge in detecting 
   colonial language effects.

Methodological Notes
================================================================================

- Low-quality models (llama-3.2-3b-instruct, qwen3-1.7b) were excluded
- English advantage calculated as: 100 × (Distance_native - Distance_english) / Distance_native
- Statistical tests use Welch's correction for unequal variances
- Effect sizes reported using Cohen's d (t-tests) and eta-squared (ANOVA)
- All confidence intervals are 95%
