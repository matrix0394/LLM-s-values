Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly used to address grand societal challenges through human-AI partnership or as social actors, yet knowledge of human value embedded in these models is nascent. Here we conduct a series of studies to extract human values across 23 LLMs and 66 countries (representing 62% of world population). We present four key results using the human values identified by the World Value Survey (WVS) Wave 7 as the benchmark. First, for most countries, human values in LLMs are more inclined to Secular-rational and self-expression values, compared with human values identified through WVS. Second, there are significant differences in LLMs' human values between using a country's native language and English. Third, for non-western countries, human values in LLMs reflects tangible evidence of the constructive otherness from a occidental perspective. Forth, a country or a region's human values in LLMs are also associated with its colonial history. We discuss the implications of our findings for the future evolution of LLMs and their involvement in real-world scenarios.
