As artificial intelligence systems become embedded in global communication, their ability to represent cultural diversity has critical implications for equity and trust. This study identifies a systematic asymmetry in how large language models express cultural values across languages: non-Western societies are represented more accurately under English prompts than under their native languages, whereas the opposite pattern holds for Western cultures. This form of digital Orientalism reflects structural biases in multilingual representation rather than simple translation errors. Our findings show that translating AI systems into local languages alone is insufficient to ensure culturally appropriate value expression, highlighting the need for more balanced approaches to multilingual training, evaluation, and deployment.
