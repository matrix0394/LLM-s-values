# Significance Statement (PNAS Format)

**Word count: 118 words**

As artificial intelligence systems become embedded in global communication, their ability to represent cultural diversity has critical implications for equity and trust. This study reveals a systematic asymmetry in how large language models encode cultural knowledge: non-Western societies are represented more accurately in English than in their native languages, whereas the opposite pattern holds for Western cultures. This pattern of digital Orientalism suggests that AI systems inherit and potentially amplify historical inequalities in global knowledge production. Our findings demonstrate that language translation alone cannot ensure culturally appropriate AI behavior and highlight the need for fundamentally rethinking how multilingual AI systems are trained, evaluated, and deployed worldwide.
